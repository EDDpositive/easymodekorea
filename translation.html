Check your own setup
Step by step, first time
  1. Install Papago, Naver's translation app. It works without an account.
  2. Before you fly, download the Korean offline pack in Papago's settings so it works without data.
  3. Menus and signs: open the camera mode, point it at the text, and read the translation over the image. You can also translate a photo from your gallery.
  4. Talking to someone: use the conversation mode. Speak in short, simple sentences, one at a time.
  5. Show, don't speak: type what you need and turn the phone so the other person can read the Korean.
  6. Reviews and place names: Naver Map translates many reviews and names in the app, so you often don't need to copy text across.
Common problems and fixes

  • The translation stops working underground.

    You haven't downloaded the offline pack, or the offline mode doesn't cover that feature. Download it on Wi-Fi.

  • The camera translation is jumbled.

    Hold the phone steady and closer, and translate one section of the menu at a time.

  • A dish name comes out as nonsense.

    Dish names often translate literally. Search the Korean dish name for a photo, or ask staff to point to a picture.

  • The other person can't understand the voice translation.

    Rephrase in a shorter sentence, or type it and show the screen.

Questions people ask

Papago or Google Translate?

Both work in Korea. Many visitors find Papago more natural for Korean, so install both and compare.

Does Papago need a Korean phone number?

No. You can use it without signing in.

Does camera translation work offline?

Some features need data. Test camera mode in flight mode at home to see what works offline.

Is there a phone line that interprets for me?

Yes. 1330, the travel helpline, can interpret over the phone, 24 hours.
