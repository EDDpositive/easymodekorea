## Mobile data and a phone number
Question: Do I need a Korean phone number in Korea?
Guide: https://easymodekorea.com/mobile-data-sim.html
Most visitors use a data eSIM. That covers maps and messaging, but Korean apps often ask for a Korean number or a full identity check. Here is what each setup can do.
- Visitor with no Korean phone number and a foreign card: Data works anywhere. Apps that send a Korean SMS code will not reach you.
  * Buy a Korea eSIM before you fly for data.
  * Pick a plan with a 010 number if you want Korean SMS codes.
  If that fails: A Korean number still won't pass full identity checks (본인인증) without an ARC. Every answer on this page already assumes that.
- Visitor with a Korean prepaid SIM (010 number): Korean SMS codes work. Full identity checks (본인인증) usually still fail.
  * Use the 010 number for SMS sign-ups.
  * Expect PASS-style identity checks to fail without an ARC.
  If that fails: Use passport verification where a service offers it, such as Naver.
- Resident with an ARC (alien registration card): A number in your own name passes most identity checks.
  * Set up PASS identity verification with your carrier.
  * Most Korean apps open up after that.
  If that fails: If a check fails, confirm your number is registered to your ARC with your carrier.

## Airport to the city
Question: How do I get from Incheon Airport to Seoul?
Guide: https://easymodekorea.com/incheon-airport-to-seoul.html
Trains, airport buses and visitor taxi apps all work without a Korean number or a Korean card.
- Everyone: Every option works for visitors. The AREX train is the simplest.
  * AREX Express: non-stop to Seoul Station, about 43 minutes from Terminal 1 and 51 from Terminal 2. Buy the ticket at the machine or counter.
  * AREX all-stop train: about an hour, stops at Hongik Univ. and Gongdeok. Tap a T-money card.
  * Airport bus: goes to major hotels and areas. Ask at the bus desk in arrivals for your route number.
  * Taxi: k.ride or Uber, or the taxi stand outside arrivals.
  If that fails: Trains don't run all night. After the last train, take a taxi or a late-night airport bus.

## Getting around: maps and transit directions
Question: Does Google Maps work in Korea?
Guide: https://easymodekorea.com/maps.html
Google Maps shows places in Korea, but its directions are limited. Korean map apps give full transit and walking directions in English.
- Everyone: Naver Map works for everyone, in English, Japanese and Chinese.
  * Install Naver Map and switch the language in settings.
  * Use it for walking, subway and bus directions.
  If that fails: Kakao Map is a good second map. Google Maps shows places, but its walking and driving directions are limited in Korea.

## Calling a taxi
Question: Can I call a taxi in Korea without a Korean number?
Guide: https://easymodekorea.com/taxi.html
Kakao T is the app most Koreans use, but visitors often get stuck on card setup. Apps built for visitors take a foreign number and card.
- Visitor with no Korean phone number and a foreign card: Use the taxi apps built for visitors. They take a foreign number and card.
  * k.ride (by Kakao Mobility): sign up with Google, Apple or email.
  * Uber also works in Korea with your existing account.
  * TABA is another visitor app, run with Seoul and T-money.
  If that fails: Kakao T is what locals use, but reports on foreign cards in Kakao T disagree. If card setup fails, choose pay-to-driver, or hail on the street.
- Visitor with a Korean prepaid SIM (010 number): k.ride, Uber or TABA are the reliable picks.
  * k.ride or Uber with your card.
  * Kakao T may accept your 010 number for sign-up.
  If that fails: If Kakao T rejects your card, pick pay-to-driver and pay in the taxi.
- Resident with an ARC (alien registration card): Kakao T works fully with a Korean number and Korean card.
  * Kakao T for the most cars.
  * k.ride or Uber as backups.
  If that fails: Street hailing works everywhere in big cities.

## Subway and bus fares (T-money)
Question: Can I top up T-money with a foreign card?
Guide: https://easymodekorea.com/tmoney-subway-bus.html
Korean subways and buses run on transit cards. Topping one up with a foreign card is now partly possible on iPhone. Cash always works.
- Visitor with no Korean phone number and a foreign card, ios: On iPhone, Mobile T-money can now top up with some foreign cards. Not every card brand is supported.
  * Install Mobile T-money and add a transit card.
  * Try topping up with your card. If your card brand isn't accepted, use the backup below.
  If that fails: Buy a physical T-money card at a convenience store and top it up with cash at the counter or station machines.
- Visitor with no Korean phone number and a foreign card, android: Foreign-card top-up in the Android T-money app was not available at our last check.
  * Buy a physical T-money card at a convenience store.
  * Top up with cash at the counter or at station machines.
  If that fails: T-money says a separate app, Mobile Korea Tour Card, supports foreign cards.
- Visitor with a Korean prepaid SIM (010 number), ios: On iPhone, Mobile T-money can now top up with some foreign cards. Not every card brand is supported.
  * Install Mobile T-money and add a transit card.
  * Try topping up with your card. If your card brand isn't accepted, use the backup below.
  If that fails: Buy a physical T-money card at a convenience store and top it up with cash at the counter or station machines.
- Visitor with a Korean prepaid SIM (010 number), android: Foreign-card top-up in the Android T-money app was not available at our last check.
  * Buy a physical T-money card at a convenience store.
  * Top up with cash at the counter or at station machines.
  If that fails: T-money says a separate app, Mobile Korea Tour Card, supports foreign cards.
- Resident with an ARC (alien registration card): With a Korean card, mobile or physical T-money works normally.
  * Add T-money in your wallet app or use a transit-enabled Korean card.
  If that fails: Physical T-money with cash always works.

## Booking KTX trains
Question: How do I book a KTX train as a foreigner?
Guide: https://easymodekorea.com/ktx-train-booking.html
Korea's rail booking changed a lot in 2026: a new app, SRT folded into KTX, and a longer booking window. Here is the current way to book.
- Everyone: Book on KORAIL's English website with a foreign card. It's the most reliable channel.
  * Use KORAIL's English booking site with your passport details.
  * Tickets open two months ahead (since 1 Oct 2026). Book early for weekends and holidays.
  If that fails: If your card fails online, try the KORAIL+ app, a reseller like Klook, or a station counter.

## Restaurant bookings and waitlists
Question: Can I book restaurants in Korea without a Korean number?
Guide: https://easymodekorea.com/restaurant-reservations.html
Popular Korean restaurants run bookings and waitlists through apps that used to need a Korean number. Two routes now work for visitors.
- Visitor with no Korean phone number and a foreign card: CatchTable Global and Naver passport verification both work without a Korean number.
  * CatchTable Global: sign up by email, pay deposits with a foreign card.
  * Deposits in the global app are usually not refundable. Check the date before you pay.
  * Naver Map bookings: verify with your passport (available since 4 Jun 2026).
  If that fails: Not every restaurant is on these apps. Ask staff to add you to the waitlist in person.
- Visitor with a Korean prepaid SIM (010 number): CatchTable Global and Naver passport verification both work without a Korean number.
  * CatchTable Global: sign up by email, pay deposits with a foreign card.
  * Deposits in the global app are usually not refundable. Check the date before you pay.
  * Naver Map bookings: verify with your passport (available since 4 Jun 2026).
  If that fails: Not every restaurant is on these apps. Ask staff to add you to the waitlist in person.
- Resident with an ARC (alien registration card): All booking apps work, including the local CatchTable app and Naver.
  * Naver Map or CatchTable with your verified number.
  If that fails: CatchTable Global if the local app gives you trouble.

## Food delivery
Question: Can I order food delivery in Korea as a tourist?
Guide: https://easymodekorea.com/food-delivery.html
Baemin, Korea's biggest delivery app, now works in English, takes a foreign phone number for its code, and accepts foreign cards.
- Visitor with no Korean phone number and a foreign card: Baemin works without a Korean number. It runs in English and takes foreign cards.
  * Install Baemin and set the app to English.
  * Enter your home phone number to get the code, or order as a guest.
  * Pay with a foreign card, or Apple Pay with a foreign card on iPhone.
  If that fails: If an order fails, your host or front desk can usually order for you.
- Visitor with a Korean prepaid SIM (010 number): Baemin works without a Korean number. It runs in English and takes foreign cards.
  * Install Baemin and set the app to English.
  * Enter your home phone number to get the code, or order as a guest.
  * Pay with a foreign card, or Apple Pay with a foreign card on iPhone.
  If that fails: If an order fails, your host or front desk can usually order for you.
- Resident with an ARC (alien registration card): Baemin, Coupang Eats and Yogiyo all work with a verified Korean number.
  * Sign up with your number and add a Korean card or pay app.
  If that fails: Baemin also has an English mode if you prefer it.

## Shopping and tax refunds
Question: How do I get a tax refund when shopping in Korea?
Guide: https://easymodekorea.com/tax-free-shopping.html
Visitors get most of the 10% tax back on shopping. At many stores it comes off at the till when you show your passport.
- Visitor with no Korean phone number and a foreign card: Tax refunds work for visitors. Show your passport at the till and the tax comes off right away at many stores.
  * Look for the Tax Free or Tax Refund sign, and bring your actual passport.
  * Spend at least 15,000 won in one payment. Receipts can't be added together, so pay for everything at once.
  * Instant refund at the till works for payments under 1 million won, up to 5 million won in total per trip.
  * No instant refund? Keep the refund receipt and claim it at a city refund booth or the airport kiosk before you check in your bags.
  If that fails: Refund receipts expire 3 months after purchase. Cosmetic and skin clinic treatments no longer qualify from 2026.
- Visitor with a Korean prepaid SIM (010 number): Tax refunds work for visitors. Show your passport at the till and the tax comes off right away at many stores.
  * Look for the Tax Free or Tax Refund sign, and bring your actual passport.
  * Spend at least 15,000 won in one payment. Receipts can't be added together, so pay for everything at once.
  * Instant refund at the till works for payments under 1 million won, up to 5 million won in total per trip.
  * No instant refund? Keep the refund receipt and claim it at a city refund booth or the airport kiosk before you check in your bags.
  If that fails: Refund receipts expire 3 months after purchase. Cosmetic and skin clinic treatments no longer qualify from 2026.
- Resident with an ARC (alien registration card): Tourist tax refunds are for visitors staying under 6 months. Residents with an ARC usually don't qualify.
  * Shop as usual. Prices already include tax.
  If that fails: If you've been in Korea less than 6 months, ask the store whether you still qualify.

## Luggage storage and delivery
Question: Where can I store luggage in Seoul?
Guide: https://easymodekorea.com/luggage-storage.html
Before check-in or after check-out, you can leave bags at subway lockers or staffed counters, or send them to your hotel or the airport.
- Everyone: Lockers and staffed luggage counters work for everyone. You can also send bags to your hotel or the airport the same day.
  * T-Locker: lockers in about 270 Seoul subway stations, from small bags to suitcases. Pay at the locker or in the T-Locker app.
  * T-Luggage: staffed counters at Seoul Station, Hongik Univ., Myeongdong, Jamsil and other busy stations store bags and deliver them to hotels or the airport.
  * Same-day delivery between the airport and Seoul hotels usually needs you to drop bags off before late morning.
  If that fails: Lockers full or the app won't work? Use a staffed counter, or ask your host or hotel to hold your bags.

## Sick or hurt
Question: What do I do if I get sick in Korea?
Guide: https://easymodekorea.com/pharmacy-hospital.html
Emergency help in Korea is free to call and has interpreters. For smaller problems, pharmacies and big hospitals' international clinics are easy to use.
- Everyone: Call 119 for an ambulance. If you're not sure where to go, call 1339, any time.
  * Emergency: call 119 and say "English, please" for an interpreter.
  * Not sure how serious it is, or need the nearest open ER or pharmacy? Call 1339. It runs 24 hours.
  * Minor problems: pharmacies (look for the 약 sign) sell common medicines. Convenience stores have basic painkillers and cold medicine.
  * Major Seoul hospitals have international clinics with English- and Chinese-speaking staff.
  If that fails: Bring your passport. An ER may ask for a deposit before treatment.

## Emergency and help numbers
- 112: Police
- 119: Fire and ambulance
- 1330: Travel helpline, 24h in English. From a foreign SIM: +82-2-1330
- 1339: Medical advice and the nearest open ER, 24h
