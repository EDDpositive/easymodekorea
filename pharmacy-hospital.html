What do I do if I get sick in Korea?

Good to go

Good to go

Call 119 for an ambulance, and also for medical advice or the nearest open ER. Call 1330 if you need an interpreter.

Where

Step by step, first time

  1. Emergency: call 119 and say "English, please" for an interpreter.
  2. Not sure how serious it is, or need the nearest open ER or pharmacy? Call 119 too. It gives medical advice and hospital information, not only ambulances, 24 hours a day.
  3. Minor problems: pharmacies (look for the 약 sign) sell common medicines. Convenience stores have basic painkillers and cold medicine.
  4. Major Seoul hospitals have international clinics with English- and Chinese-speaking staff.

If that failsBring your passport. An ER may ask for a deposit before treatment.

Step by step, first time
  1. Emergency: call 119 for an ambulance. Say your language ("English, please") to get an interpreter.
  2. Not sure how serious it is, or need the nearest open ER or pharmacy? Call 119 too. It gives medical advice and hospital information, 24 hours.
  3. Need an interpreter at a pharmacy or clinic? Call 1330, the travel helpline, and hand over the phone.
  4. Minor problems: go to a pharmacy (look for the 약 sign). Pharmacists can suggest common medicines; antibiotics need a doctor's prescription.
  5. Seeing a doctor: big Seoul hospitals have international clinics with English-speaking staff. Small clinics are cheaper and quicker for simple problems.
  6. Bring your passport and a payment card. An ER may ask for a deposit before treatment.
  7. For travel insurance, ask for an itemised receipt and a diagnosis note in English before you leave.
Common problems and fixes

  • The pharmacy is closed at night.

    Call 119 for the nearest open pharmacy or ER. Convenience stores sell basic painkillers and cold medicine.

  • Staff don't speak your language.

    Call 1330 and ask them to interpret, or show a translation in Papago.

  • The ER asks for a deposit.

    Pay with a card and keep every receipt for your insurer.

  • Your insurer wants documents later.

    Ask the hospital for an itemised receipt and a diagnosis note in English before you leave; getting them later is harder.

Questions people ask

Is medical care expensive for visitors?

Without Korean health insurance you pay the full price, so travel insurance is worth having. Simple clinic visits are usually affordable.

Can I buy antibiotics at a pharmacy?

No. Antibiotics need a doctor's prescription in Korea.

What is 1339 for?

It is the disease-control hotline (KDCA), mainly for infectious diseases. For emergencies, call 119.

Do I need to bring my passport?

Yes. Hospitals use it to register you.
